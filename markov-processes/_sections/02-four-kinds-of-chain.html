{{S}}Four kinds of chain

"Markov process" names a family, not a model. Two independent choices — whether time ticks or flows, and whether the state is countable or not — produce four families, and each one owns a different corner of computer science. Two further extensions change the index set or add a decision, and both keep the name.

Discrete time, discrete state

The default meaning of "Markov chain". The transition law is a finite table, which is why this cell is where computing spends most of its time: a table can be stored, inverted, raised to a power, and learned by counting. Everything in §3 lives here.

Discrete time, continuous state

The state is a vector and the "matrix" becomes a kernel: a probability density over where you land next. Two special cases carry most of the weight. If the transition is linear and the noise Gaussian, the whole filtering problem has a closed form — that is the Kalman filter (§8). If the transition is "add a calibrated amount of Gaussian noise", you have the forward process of a diffusion model (§12).

Continuous time, discrete state

Events happen at instants rather than on a clock: a request arrives, a disk fails, a node learns a rumour. The describing object is not a matrix of probabilities but a matrix of rates, the generator Q, and the arithmetic goes through a matrix exponential. This is the cell performance engineering and reliability engineering both live in, and §4 is about why memorylessness forces the exponential distribution on you here.

Continuous time, continuous state

Both limits taken at once: diffusions, described by stochastic differential equations. Computationally you almost always discretise back into the cell above it, which is why Langevin dynamics appears both as an SDE in the theory and as a loop with a step size in the code.

And two things that are not chains but keep the name

A Markov random field replaces the timeline with a graph. The property becomes: a node is conditionally independent of all others given its neighbours. That is the assumption under image denoising, the Ising model, and conditional random fields — and the "Markov blanket" of a node in a Bayesian network is the same idea named differently.

A Markov decision process inserts a choice into the transition, P(s′ | s, a). The process stops being something you observe and becomes something you steer, which is the entire subject of §9. Add partial observability on top and you get a POMDP, where the Markov state is no longer visible and you must carry a belief instead — the subject of §5.
