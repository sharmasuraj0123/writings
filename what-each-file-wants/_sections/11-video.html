One clip, three encoders

The clip is frames 480–719 of Xiph's 1080p24 Sintel trailer: computer animation, letterboxed to 2.35:1, 8-bit 4:2:0 (How the measurements were made). x264 and x265 ran at preset medium and SVT-AV1 at its default preset 8, four CRFs each (constant rate factor: each encoder's constant-quality setting, where lower means higher quality and more bits; the scales differ between encoders) from the docs, all else at defaults; VP9 was not run. VMAF is FFmpeg's default model, vmaf_v0.6.1, averaged over all 240 frames; the VMAF v1 models were not run. measured here What VMAF and BD-rate measure is in Throwing away what nobody sees.

BD-rate follows draft-ietf-netvc-testing-09 §4.2: a piecewise cubic Hermite fit of log-rate against quality, integrated by the trapezoidal rule over at least 1,000 samples. For different codecs the draft bounds the range with a third, anchor codec. It asks for at least four points per codec. from the spec Each encoder here has four. With no anchor, the range is the VMAF both curves cover; against x264, three points of each curve fall inside it. x265 needed 39.09% fewer bits than x264, and SVT-AV1 at preset 8 needed 34.56% fewer. SVT-AV1 against x265 is withheld: only two of its points fall inside x265's range, CRF 55 missing it by 0.007 VMAF. The saving shrinks as quality rises: x265's from 41.6% at VMAF 90 to 35.7% at 95, preset 8's from 39.9% to 28.3%, below x265's at each level (Figure {{fig-rd}}). measured here

On this clip, the preset mattered as much as the codec. At CRF 35, preset 4 scored VMAF 95.70 at 1,341.7 kbps: 34.3% fewer bits than preset 8 needs for that VMAF and 21.3% fewer than x265, in x265's speed tier of 3–10 fps. Preset 10 cost 24.1% more bits than 8. These are single points against interpolated curves, not BD-rates. measured here

What to do

Netflix's Anne Aaron, in AOMedia's IBC 2026 release (September 2026), said AV1 "now powers over a third of our global VOD streaming".
