{{S}}JSON: pick a level, then stop

Every codec here shrinks JSON; they differ in what the last few per cent cost. Past a knee, each level adds little ratio and often costs a tier of compression speed, while decompression stays where it was. Find the knee, set the level there, and stop.

Three files, twenty-three settings

The corpora are this site's posts.json (27,667 bytes when measured), the _numbers.json of What the Parser Hears (518,267) and 4,100 synthetic API order records (4,926,885 minified). measured here The records draw customers and products from fixed, skewed pools of 1,500 and 400, so values repeat across records (_compute.py, lines 434–436). from the tree Each corpus is also re-serialised three ways: indented two spaces, Python's default json.dumps, and minified. Every form goes through 23 settings of gzip (zlib, zlib-ng, libdeflate), Brotli, zstd, xz and LZ4; every output is decoded and compared. Speeds come from the API records alone and are tiers (How the measurements were made). measured here

Where the knee is

In Figure {{fig-json-frontier}}, zstd 3 gives 8.03× and compresses in the 100–500 MB/s tier; gzip -6 gives 6.07× a tier lower, so zstd 3's output is 24.46% smaller. zstd 3 to 9 adds 25.34% to the ratio and drops one tier; 9 to 19 adds 14.13% and drops two, to below 1 MB/s; 19 to 22 adds 0.08%. Brotli bends sooner: quality 1 to 5 adds 63.04%, 5 to 9 adds 7.72%, and 9 to 11 adds 10.56%, each a tier slower. gzip -6 to -9 adds 4.98% inside its tier. measured here

Decoding speed follows the codec more than the level. The zstd README says decompression "remains roughly the same at all settings". from the docs Here every zstd level decoded at 500 MB/s or more and every Brotli quality below it; indented, zstd 3 and Brotli 5 to 11 swapped sides, so that boundary is not stable. measured here zlib-ng and libdeflate write the same gzip format as zlib: at level 6 their output is within 3.74% of zlib's, either way by corpus, and their own decoders run a tier faster. measured here

Defaults, and other people's numbers

zstd 3 is not denser than gzip -6 everywhere. On both real files, as found, its output is larger: 7,807 against 7,387 bytes for posts.json (+5.69%), and 49,754 against 47,037 for the parser post's file (+5.78%). GNU gzip 1.14 and the separate zstandard binding give the same order. zstd 9 beat gzip -6 on all three corpora: by 1.49% and 15.74% on the files as found, and 39.73% on the minified records. measured here At level 3, zstd's dependable lead is speed. reconstruction

The brotli command line defaults to quality 11, its slowest, and zstd to level 3 (The codecs that matter in 2026). from the docs On the records, brotli 11's output is 32.17% smaller than zstd 3's, written below 1 MB/s; matched by tier, brotli 5 is 1.25% larger than zstd 9, and brotli 11 2.97% smaller than zstd 19. measured here The 20–26% Brotli gain quoted against gzip was Google's 2015 figure against Zopfli. from the docs Against gzip -9, brotli 11 saved 17.87% on posts.json and 26.77% on the parser post's file, both as found, and 46.21% on the minified records. measured here

On minified SchemaStore documents of 100 bytes and up, Viotti and Kinderkhedia, in an unrefereed preprint, found GZIP's average and median reductions the best of GZIP, LZ4 and LZMA ("A Benchmark of JSON-compatible Binary Serialization Specifications", arXiv:2201.03051, 2022, §8.4.1). from the docs Here xz -6 beat gzip -6 on all three minified files, by 3.98% to 47.31%. measured here A ranking travels only with its inputs. reconstruction Their tiny documents, and Facebook's 2016 dictionary, are in Small messages and dictionaries.

Indented or minified

RFC 8259 §2: "Insignificant whitespace is allowed before or after any of the six structural characters." Minifying loses no data. from the spec Python's json.dumps is not minimal by default: without indent it puts a space after each comma and colon, and its documentation points to separators=(',', ':') for the most compact output. from the docs That default form, which also escapes non-ASCII, makes the records 9.53% larger than minified, and 1.52% larger after zstd 3. measured here

Compression removes most of what indentation adds: minifying the indented records saves 34.99% raw but 4.79% after brotli 11 (Figure {{fig-minify}}). Ratios flatter indentation: indented, the records reach 11.04× under zstd 3 against 8.03× minified, yet compress to 686,355 bytes against 613,261. measured here Compare bytes, not ratios. reconstruction

What to set

My reading of the measurements above: reconstruction

  • At rest, written often: zstd 3, which compressed both forms of the records at 100 MB/s or more.
  • Read often, or replacing gzip -6: zstd 9, at the knee: gzip -6's compression tier, smaller on all three corpora, a decode tier faster.
  • Cold archives: zstd 19, which still decoded at 500 MB/s or more. Skip --ultra: it unlocks levels 20 to 22 and needs more memory to compress and to decode from the docs; level 22 added at most 1.20% to any ratio here. measured here
  • At the edge: brotli 11 for static files, brotli 5 or zstd 3 per request; negotiation is in Compression on the wire.
  • Only gzip: level 6, not nginx's default gzip_comp_level of 1 from the docs; gzip -1 wrote 11.18% to 47.79% more bytes than -6. measured here
