Ten rules

1. Decide by the data and its reads, not by a codec's fame. zstd 3 wrote 5.7% more bytes than gzip -6 on posts.json and 24.5% fewer on the API records, and gave one audit log 2.08× in 1 KiB blocks, 8.68× in 1 MiB (Logs). measured here

2. At rest, zstd 3 (9 if read often, 19 for cold archives); at the edge, brotli or zstd. zstd 3 compressed the API records at 100–500 MB/s; zstd 9, in gzip -6's tier, beat gzip -6 on all three JSON corpora; zstd 19 still decoded at 500 MB/s or more; brotli 11, the densest, compressed below 1 MB/s (JSON). measured here

3. Never compress a tiny payload alone: batch it, or use a versioned dictionary. gzip -6 grew 1,216 of 1,409 synthetic messages under 200 bytes. zstd 3 gave 1.98× per message, 3.27× in batches of 100 and 3.21× per message with a trained dictionary (Small messages). measured here

4. Give a table a columnar format before a codec. Parquet's encodings alone cut a synthetic 124.5 MB CSV to 38.6 MB; adding zstd 3 made it 23.5 MB, 35.2% smaller than the CSV under gzip -6 (Tables). measured here

5. For media, choose codec and quality, measure, keep the source. At an SSIMULACRA 2 score of 80, AVIF took 34.5% fewer bytes than libjpeg-turbo's 4:2:0 JPEG on three photographs (Images). measured here Match the score, not the setting; cut lossy copies from the master (Throwing away what nobody sees). reconstruction

6. Leave compressed data alone. Four general-purpose codecs saved at most 4.66% on nine media files. Re-coding pays: oxipng took 40.1% off a screenshot, and JPEG XL 32.8% off a JPEG it rebuilt bit for bit (Already compressed). measured here

7. Record codec, level and dictionary with the data. A zstd frame header has a field for a dictionary ID, none for the level (RFC 8878 §3.1.1.1). from the spec Parquet names each column chunk's codec; all 14 in the zstd 3 file read ZSTD. measured here Keep the level and the dictionary's SHA-256 in a manifest. reconstruction

8. Bound decompression by its output. Python's zlib and compression.zstd decompressors take a max_length. from the docs Decompression bombs and the YAML alias bomb are one family: few bytes in, unbounded work out. reconstruction

9. Never compress a secret with attacker-controlled input. "One should never compress a message whose content must remain secret with a message generated by a third party" (RFC 8878 §8). from the spec BREACH's authors list disabling HTTP compression first among mitigations (When compression is the attack). from the docs

10. Make archives reproducible: fixed dates, sorted entries. npm fixes every tarball entry's date (Config files). from the docs xo-space's backup sorts its paths (tarball.py, line 131), from the tree but stamps each run's time into its gzip header; the same section has the fix. reconstruction

Where this breaks

One shared machine with two CPUs of time, throttled in 40.3% of its cgroup periods, made every measurement: speeds are tiers (the bench). The API records, messages, access logs and table are synthetic. The video BD-rates, x265 −39.09% and SVT-AV1 preset 8 −34.56% against x264, rest on one 10-second animated clip, VMAF v0.6.1 and four points per encoder, three inside the shared range; lossy images on three Kodak photographs; audio has no quality score. measured here VMAF and SSIMULACRA 2 model perception; they are not perception. reconstruction Browser support moves monthly; Figure {{fig-image-support}} is dated. reconstruction

The decision rule

Ask the three questions of Figure {{fig-decision}} in order. Already entropy-coded? Store it as it is, or re-code it from its source at a measured quality. Small? Batch it or give it a dictionary. Read how: over HTTP, from disk, by offset or by column? That picks the edge codec, the zstd level, the frame size or Parquet. Record the choice. reconstruction
