{{S}}Compression is prediction

A model that predicts the next byte well leaves the coder little to pay for. The best lossless compressors are therefore the best predictors, and neural networks now win at the frontier. Headline numbers hide the model: its size, and the time to run it again at the other end.

Delétang et al. open "Language Modeling Is Compression" (ICLR 2024) with the equivalence: "It has long been established that predictive models can be transformed into lossless compressors and vice versa." from the paper With an arithmetic coder (A model and a coder), the output is the model's log loss summed over the file, plus a few bits. reconstruction

The Hutter Prize

The Hutter Prize asks for a compressor of size S1 that turns enwik9, 109 bytes of text, into a self-extracting archive of size S2. The score S = S1 + S2 must beat the record L, and pays €500,000 × (1 − S/L) for a gain of at least 1%, for a run of at most about 50 hours on one CPU core in under 10 GB of RAM (prize page, modified 30 September 2026). from the docs

The record moved three times in 2026 (Figure {{fig-hutter}}). It had stood at fx2-cmix's 110,793,128 bytes, dated 3 September 2024; cmix-lex (26 June) and cmix-obias (19 July) each cleared the 1% minimum. Then Vladimir Ivanov's fx2-cmix-T (24 July, awarded 30 September) reached 100,424,672, 7.46% below cmix-obias, and 7.46% of €500,000 is the €37,300 it won. from the docs The Large Text Compression Benchmark (LTCB), which calls it fx2-cmix-transformer, says it replaced fx2-cmix's LSTM "with a transformer with 6M parameters pre-trained on enwik9 on 8 RTX 5090 GPUs for 26 hours, adding 2.9 MB to the compressor and decompressor. It does not need a GPU to run." from the docs James Byrne's zmix write-up, quoted on LTCB: "The score counts the weights twice, once in the compressor and once in the archive". from the docs

Two scoreboards, opposite ratios

Matt Mahoney's LTCB adds "the size of a zip archive containing the decompresser" to "the compressed size of enwik9", and for a self-extracting archive "the size is 0". from the docs So fx2-cmix-T is 96,996,198 bytes there, 100,424,672 for Hutter: the gap is its 3,428,474-byte compressor. LTCB's leader, Marcus Wölk's cmix-neif 1.0 (5 October 2026), is 91,913,343 there and 95,914,051 by the Hutter formula, pending. from the docs If upheld, that is 4.49% below the record. reconstruction

LTCB's top five are transformer entries derived from cmix; the fourth, rata-cmix 1.0.0, is "not a Hutter prize candidate", and Mahoney has "not verified results submitted by others". nncp v3.2, Fabrice Bellard's GPU transformer, is ninth: 106,632,363 bytes (0.853 bits per byte) plus a 628,955-byte decompressor. paq8px, the context-mixing line, reached v217 on 28 September 2026. from the docs

Hutter's "Compr.Factor" is original ÷ compressed (9.96 for fx2-cmix-T); LTCB's headings are compressed ÷ original (.0919 for cmix-neif). from the docs Delétang et al. use compressed ÷ raw, in percent. from the paper This post divides the Hutter way. Decoding costs as much as encoding: cmix-neif took 48.72 hours to compress and 48.73 to decompress on its submitter's machine. from the docs The decoder reruns the predictor; an LZ decoder mostly copies (The codecs that matter in 2026). reconstruction

Better than PNG, without the weights

Delétang et al.'s abstract says "Chinchilla 70B, while trained primarily on text, compresses ImageNet patches to 43.4% and LibriSpeech samples to 16.4% of their raw size, beating domain-specific compressors like PNG (58.5%) or FLAC (30.3%), respectively." Its §1 adds "(not accounting for model parameter size)". from the paper Table 1, in both versions, says 48.0% and 21.0% at 2048-byte chunks. from the paper The paper's repository README repeats the abstract's figures, with no erratum. from the docs Nothing I found explains the gap. reconstruction

What this means for agents

A model's weights are a dictionary both ends already hold, like Brotli's built-in one or a trained zstd dictionary (Saying it again, by reference; measured in Small messages and dictionaries), at 140 GB instead of kilobytes. A dictionary shared in advance is fairly left out of a ratio; one that must be shipped dominates it. reconstruction Both ends must also predict identically. Bellard's ts_zip, an RWKV 169M model driving an arithmetic coder, says "The model is evaluated in a deterministic and reproducible way." from the docs

For an agent, the cost that matters is the context both ends share: the weights, and what sits in the window. When the window fills, agents do not compress it losslessly. They compact it: a summary replaces older turns, and no decoder can recover what the summariser left out (Compacting a conversation). reconstruction
