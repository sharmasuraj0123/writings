{{S}}Licence and owners

Terraform is a compiler whose intermediate representation is a dependency graph and whose back end is somebody else's process. Parts 1, 2 and 3 read that compiler from the inside. This part puts it in the world. First the ecosystem: who owns which layer, how far the OpenTofu fork has drifted, and where the commercial features run. Then a single project that builds the same stack on AWS, GCP and Azure, taken through validate, mocked tests and a full plan with no cloud credentials at all. The part, and the collection, end with the register of all 101 findings.

The thesis of the part is that Terraform's open seams are the plugin protocol and the providers, and that almost everything else, including every new commercial engine, now runs outside the core binary. This section starts with the licence, because the licence draws the seam. Its claim: the files that stayed open are exactly the ones another tool needs to run the same providers.

One commit, one file

The relicensing is a single commit. On 2023-08-10 the hashicorp-copywrite[bot] account committed b145fbcaa, which replaced LICENSE with the Business Source License 1.1 and pointed to HashiCorp's announcement. The first tag that contains it is v1.6.0 from the code. The v1.5 branch had already been cut, so v1.5.7, tagged on 2023-09-07 from that branch, is the last MPL-2.0 release, and the last MPL commit on main is 8a085b427b74, the merge of #33590.

The licence file at v1.17.0 is short, and its parameters say most of what matters (LICENSE:6-12, 43-44): 

Licensor:             International Business Machines Corporation (IBM)
Licensed Work:        Terraform Version 1.6.0 or later. The Licensed Work is (c) 2024
                      IBM Corp.
Additional Use Grant: You may make production use of the Licensed Work, provided
                      Your use does not include offering the Licensed Work to third
                      parties on a hosted or embedded basis in order to compete with
                      IBM Corp.'s paid version(s) of the Licensed Work. For purposes
…
Change Date:          Four years from the date the Licensed Work is published.
Change License:       MPL 2.0

Three details in that grant narrow it. A "competitive offering" is a product "offered to third parties on a paid basis" that "significantly overlaps" with IBM's paid versions; a product that was not competitive when it first shipped does not become competitive when IBM adds features later; and "Products that are not provided on a paid basis are not competitive" (LICENSE:15-23). Internal use is spelled out: "Hosting or using the Licensed Work(s) for internal purposes within an organization is not considered a competitive offering" (LICENSE:35-37) from the code.

The change date is per version. The licence "applies separately for each version of the Licensed Work and the Change Date may vary for each version" (LICENSE:71-73). v1.6.0 was published on 2023-10-04, so by the four-year rule it becomes MPL 2.0 around October 2027; that date is arithmetic, not a statement in the file reconstruction. Each later release starts its own four-year clock.

For a practitioner the consequence is narrow. Running Terraform for your own infrastructure, in CI, or on an internal platform is untouched. Selling hosted Terraform runs that overlap HCP Terraform is what the grant excludes.

What stayed open

The interesting count is not the licence text but the SPDX headers. At 018a99c83c5d, 1,988 files carry SPDX-License-Identifier: BUSL-1.1 and 7 carry MPL-2.0 measured here (git grep over the tag). The seven are the two protocol definitions, docs/plugin-protocol/tfplugin5.proto and tfplugin6.proto; the four generated Go stubs for them under internal/tfplugin5 and internal/tfplugin6; and one Azure backend helper, internal/backend/remote-state/azure/helpers.go. The protocol directory has its own docs/plugin-protocol/LICENSE, which begins "Copyright IBM Corp. 2014, 2026" and then "Mozilla Public License, version 2.0" from the code.

Around the core, the libraries kept their licences. HCL and terraform-plugin-framework are MPL 2.0, now headed "Copyright IBM Corp."; go-cty, the value system from Part 1, is MIT under Martin Atkins's name; and each of the four cached provider packages ships an MPL 2.0 LICENSE.txt. HashiCorp's announcement said as much in advance: the BSL would cover "all future releases" of the products, while APIs, SDKs and almost all libraries would stay MPL 2.0 from the docs.

Put the two facts together and the shape is plain. A tool that wants to drive the existing provider ecosystem needs the wire protocol, the SDKs that providers are built on, and the provider binaries. All of those are MPL. Only the thing that reads .tf files, builds the graph and writes the state is BUSL. Part 2 showed the protocol is the stable seam between core and providers; the licence makes the same cut. OpenTofu runs the same provider binaries through it. Pulumi's terraform-bridge (Apache-2.0) wraps Terraform providers, and Crossplane's Upjet generates Crossplane providers from them, including the official AWS, Azure and GCP ones from the docs.

IBM, a year later in the tree

IBM agreed on 2024-04-24 to buy HashiCorp for $35 a share in cash, an enterprise value of $6.4 billion, and completed the purchase on 2025-02-27 from the docs. The repository caught up about a year later. Radek Simko's make copyrightfix commit 0fe906fa8 on 2026-02-13 switched the source headers to "Copyright IBM Corp.", and commit 4eba7c059 on 2026-03-11 changed the Licensor line from "HashiCorp, Inc." to "International Business Machines Corporation (IBM)", with a wording follow-up in f3658552ea six days later. Both first shipped in v1.15.0 on 2026-04-29 from the code.

The commercial surface moved too. The HCP Terraform legacy Free plan reached end of life on 2026-03-31, with organizations moved to an "enhanced" free tier of 500 managed resources. The pricing page is IBM-branded and charges per resource per month: $0.10 Essentials, $0.47 Standard, $0.99 Premium. HashiCorp support articles now redirect to ibm.com/mysupport from the docs.

The fork and the archive

The fork's side of the story is OpenTofu's own. The OpenTF manifesto asked HashiCorp to return Terraform to MPL or another well-known open-source licence. With no response, the fork was created on 2023-08-25, the repository went public on 2023-09-05, and the Linux Foundation announced OpenTofu on 2023-09-20 with 140-plus organizations, 600-plus individuals and at least 18 full-time developers pledged for five years. OpenTofu 1.6.0 went GA on 2024-01-10 as a "drop-in replacement", and the project entered the CNCF Sandbox on 2025-04-23. OpenTofu also reports a HashiCorp cease-and-desist letter of 2024-04-03 over its removed block, and published a response on 2024-04-11 arguing the code came from older MPL sources; HashiCorp's side of that exchange was not found from the docs.

One HashiCorp project did not survive the period. CDKTF, the CDK for Terraform, was sunset and archived on 2025-12-10 because it "did not find product-market fit at scale"; its last release was v0.21.0 on 2025-06-04, and the documented exit is cdktf synth --hcl, which writes readable HCL from the docs. If you still generate Terraform from TypeScript or Python with CDKTF, that command is the way out.
