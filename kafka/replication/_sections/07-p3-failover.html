{{S}}Losing a controller

Timers default low on purpose: election 1000 ms, fetch 2000 ms, and the comment says standbys are hot, so leader change is cheap (raft/…/QuorumConfig.java:48-49, :77-104). The election timeout is randomised in [T, 2T) (QuorumState.java:748) from the code.

Three dedicated controllers, two brokers, default timers, milliseconds after the signal, from the first matching log line on a surviving controller (exp2_failover.sh, exp2b_probe_fence.sh) measured here:

After SIGKILL, a follower's fetch timer fired 1.6 to 2.0 s later and a new controller was active 1.9 to 2.6 s after the kill, over 7 usable rounds. Fetch timeout to leader took 116 to 435 ms. The wait for the timer is most of it. SIGSTOP looks the same to followers, at 2.1 s: a pause longer than the fetch timeout costs leadership.

SIGTERM took 527 to 610 ms, about a quarter of a kill. A graceful resignation sends EndQuorumEpoch, which shortens each follower's fetch timer by its rank, and the best-replicated voter starts at once (KafkaRaftClient.java:1305-1313). That removes the fetch timeout from the sum.

A leader has its own detector. With both followers stopped, it logged the check-quorum expiry about 3.25 s after the SIGSTOP. That matches 1.5 × fetch timeout (LeaderState.java:153), not the fetch timeout the config doc names (D24, class C, still at trunk 09f2dc1320).

What a client saw: an alter-config loop through a broker got its first success 2.4 to 2.8 s after each SIGKILL, with one failed call per round. That failure is the probe's own 1500 ms request timeout, not a count of lost writes. A SIGKILLed broker was fenced 8.2 to 9.0 s after the kill, and a SIGSTOPped one 9.7 s after, inside the 7 to 10.1 s window the session, heartbeat and sweep predict. A SIGCONTed broker came back in the same epoch within 22 ms; a restarted one re-registered with a new epoch. A pause does not cost a registration, and a restart does. No iptables here, so SIGSTOP stands in for a silent peer, not for an asymmetric partition.

The KIPs match the code on intent, and lag it on status. KIP-595 states the pull design and the reason for BeginQuorumEpoch. KIP-996 states the pre-vote rule, and its page still says "Implementing". KIP-631's timeouts differ from the code's, and code wins (D29). KIP-833 marked KRaft production-ready for new clusters in 3.3.
